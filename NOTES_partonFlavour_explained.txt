How we get the jet flavour (hadronFlavour and partonFlavour) in the Z+b / Z+c study
====================================================================================

Written 2026-10-21 for the Run 3 Z(->ll)+b / Z(->ll)+c work (workflow Zb_Zc_ratio, AK4 jets,
Summer22 DY MC). It answers three questions from the group meeting:

  1. Where does partonFlavour come from? Do we calculate it ourselves?
  2. Is this the official CMS way?
  3. Where do the "99% of b jets and 95% of c jets" on slide 21 come from, and how do
     they compare with anything official?


SHORT ANSWER
------------

- We do NOT calculate partonFlavour ourselves. Every simulated jet in NanoAOD already
  carries a number called Jet_partonFlavour, and we read that number as it is.
- CMS fills that number with its own official tool. It is the same number every CMS
  analysis gets from NanoAOD, so in that sense we ARE using it the official way.
- The only thing we do is sort the number into five groups (b, c, uds, g, none).
- The "99% / 95%" on slide 21 is NOT taken from any CMS page. We measured it ourselves on
  our 20-files-per-sample DY MC. CMS gives no official percentage to compare it with. The
  CMS page only says that the two flavour labels disagree "in rare instances".


PART 1. WHAT "JET FLAVOUR" MEANS IN SIMULATION
-----------------------------------------------

A jet is a spray of particles that starts from one quark or gluon. In real data we never
know which quark or gluon started a jet. In simulation (MC) we do know, because the
generator keeps a record of every particle it made.

CMS uses that record to give each simulated jet a "truth" label. There are two labels, and
NanoAOD stores both of them for every jet:

  Jet_hadronFlavour   based on HADRONS (the particles a quark turns into)
  Jet_partonFlavour   based on PARTONS (the quarks and gluons themselves)

Both labels are produced by the same CMS tool and in the same way. Only the input differs:
one looks at hadrons, the other looks at partons.


PART 2. HOW CMS PUTS THE LABEL ON A JET (THE "GHOST" TRICK)
------------------------------------------------------------

The problem: you have a jet and a list of quarks/hadrons from the generator. Which ones
"belong" to the jet?

The old way was to draw a circle around the jet and see what falls inside. CMS stopped
doing that because the answer depends on the circle size you choose.

The current way (called "ghost association") works like this:

  Step 1. Take the generator particles you care about (B hadrons, C hadrons, or quarks and
          gluons).
  Step 2. Shrink their energy to almost zero (multiply it by 1e-18). Their direction stays
          the same. They are now "ghosts": too weak to change the jet in any way.
  Step 3. Run the jet-building algorithm again with the ghosts mixed in with the real
          particles.
  Step 4. Each ghost ends up inside exactly one jet, or in no jet at all. Look at which
          ghosts landed inside your jet.

Because the ghosts carry no energy, the jets come out exactly the same as before. The only
new information is which ghosts each jet picked up. There is no circle and no size to choose.

Rules for hadronFlavour (from ghost HADRONS):
  5  (b)      the jet contains at least one B hadron
  4  (c)      it contains a C hadron but no B hadron
  0  (light)  it contains neither

Rules for partonFlavour (from ghost QUARKS and GLUONS):
  5  (b)      the jet contains at least one b quark
  4  (c)      it contains a c quark but no b quark
  1, 2, 3     only light quarks (d, u, s): the jet gets the flavour of the most energetic one
  21          same as above, but the most energetic one is a gluon
  0           no quark or gluon landed in the jet at all ("undefined")
  (A minus sign means an antiquark, for example -5 is an anti-b.)

The two labels are worked out separately. Neither is allowed to overwrite the other (in the
CMS settings: hadronFlavourHasPriority = False). That is why they can disagree for a few
jets (see Part 5).


PART 3. WHERE THIS IS WRITTEN DOWN OFFICIALLY
---------------------------------------------

a) The CMS explanation page (public TWiki), section "Hadron parton based jet flavour":
   https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMSPublic/SWGuideBTagMCTools#Hadron_parton_based_jet_flavour
   It gives the rules in Part 2 and says:
     - "the main use case for the hadron-based flavour is b tagging"
     - the hadron-based flavour "cannot be used to distinguish different types of
       light-flavour jets" (that is what partonFlavour is for: gluon vs u/d/s)
     - "In rare instances a conflict between the hadron- and parton-based flavours can
       occur." No numbers are given.

b) The CMS software that writes the NanoAOD branch (CMSSW 13_0_X, used for Summer22):
   - PhysicsTools/NanoAOD/python/jetMC_cff.py
       partonFlavour = Var("partonFlavour()", "int16", doc="flavour from parton matching")
       hadronFlavour = Var("hadronFlavour()", "uint8", doc="flavour from hadron ghost clustering")
     NanoAOD just copies the value already stored on the MiniAOD jet.
   - PhysicsTools/PatAlgos/python/mcMatchLayer0/jetFlavourId_cff.py
       the module "patJetFlavourAssociation" is the tool "JetFlavourClustering" (the ghost
       method of Part 2) with anti-kT, R = 0.4, ghostRescaling = 1e-18,
       hadronFlavourHasPriority = False.
   - PhysicsTools/PatAlgos/python/producersLayer1/jetProducer_cfi.py
       useLegacyJetMCFlavour = False, so the ghost method is used (the old circle method
       is only switched on for heavy-ion running).
   - Run 3 NanoAOD jets are PUPPI jets ("slimmedJetsPuppi"). They are made in
     PhysicsTools/PatAlgos/python/slimming/miniAOD_tools.py with addJetCollection(...,
     labelName='Puppi', algo='AK', rParam=0.4). In PhysicsTools/PatAlgos/python/tools/
     jetTools.py, addJetCollection makes a copy of the same patJetFlavourAssociation
     (ghost method) for these jets. So PUPPI jets get the same official labels.

   Note: the description text says "flavour from parton matching". That wording is left
   over from the old method. The value really comes from the ghost method above.

c) Checked in our own input file (2026-10-21), a Summer22 DY NanoAODv12 file:
     /store/mc/Run3Summer22NanoAODv12/DYto2L-2Jets_MLL-50_TuneCP5_13p6TeV_amcatnloFXFX-pythia8/

PART 4. WHAT OUR CODE DOES
--------------------------

File: src/BTVNanoCommissioning/workflows/Zb_Zc_ratio.py

For the leading AK4 jet in each selected event (pT > 30 GeV, |eta| < 2.5, jet ID,
at least 0.4 away from both leptons), the code does two things:

  Main label (hadronFlavour, function jet_flavour):
      hadronFlavour == 5  ->  "b"
      hadronFlavour == 4  ->  "c"
      anything else       ->  "l" (light)

  Extra label for comparison (partonFlavour, function parton_flavour):
      take the number without its sign (anti-b counts as b, and so on), then
      5        ->  "b"
      4        ->  "c"
      1, 2, 3  ->  "uds"
      21       ->  "g"
      0        ->  "none"

That is all. No circles, no matching, no thresholds of our own. We do not change any
jet's value. We only put the official numbers into groups.

Our own choices (worth saying out loud):
  - We ignore the sign, so quark and antiquark are treated the same.
  - We put d, u and s into one group "uds".
  - We only look at the leading jet in the event.
  - For the Z+b / Z+c result itself we use hadronFlavour, as BTV recommends.
    partonFlavour is only a cross-check, plus a way to split light jets into gluon and uds.

So the answer to "are we doing partonFlavour the official way?" is: the label itself is
the official CMS one, read straight from NanoAOD. The grouping above is the only part that
is ours, and it is a plain relabelling, not a new definition.


PART 5. THE NUMBERS ON SLIDE 21 (OUR OWN MEASUREMENT)
-----------------------------------------------------

Question asked: "for jets that hadronFlavour calls b (or c), how often does partonFlavour
also call them b (or c)?"

Sample: our 20-files-per-sample Summer22 DY MC run, Z->ee and Z->mumu added together.
Output file: hists_zbzc_ak4_20files/merged/zbzc_ak4_20files.coffea

"Raw" = plain count of MC events. "Weighted" = after cross-section weights, which is
what the slide uses.

  hadronFlavour b jets (raw 82,303 events)
                     partonFlavour:   b        c       uds      g      none
     raw                            99.40%   0.05%   0.34%   0.21%   0.00%
     weighted                       99.31%   0.13%   0.15%   0.42%   0.00%

  hadronFlavour c jets (raw 151,037 events)
                     partonFlavour:   b        c       uds      g      none
     raw                             0.28%  94.17%   3.66%   1.89%   0.00%
     weighted                        0.10%  95.27%   2.81%   1.82%   0.01%

  hadronFlavour light jets (raw 1,585,032 events)
                     partonFlavour:   b        c       uds      g      none
     raw                             0.11%   1.04%  71.30%  27.52%   0.03%
     weighted                        0.15%   1.03%  67.01%  31.31%   0.50%

So the slide's "99% of b jets and 95% of c jets" are the weighted numbers 99.31% and
95.27%, rounded. Zee and Zmm agree with each other to within half a percent
(c: 95.5% Zee, 95.1% Zmm).

We checked these numbers two independent ways and they agree:
  - from a plain event counter filled in the workflow ("nevt_migration", raw column)
  - from two different weighted histograms (jet_pt, used by the slide, and z_mass)

The older 1-file-per-sample deck shows 92% for c instead of 95%. That is just because it
had too few events (Z->mumu alone came out at 90.5%). Use the 20-file number.

Careful with the direction. The numbers above start from hadronFlavour. If you start from
partonFlavour instead ("of jets that partonFlavour calls c, how many does hadronFlavour
call c?"), you get about 90% instead of 95%. Most of the difference comes from light jets
(no C hadron) that still picked up a c quark ghost. Light jets are so common that even 1% of
them is a lot of jets.


PART 6. HOW DIFFERENT ARE WE FROM "OFFICIAL"?
---------------------------------------------

- The flavour values: no difference at all. We read the official CMS values unchanged.
- The agreement percentage (99% / 95%): CMS publishes no official number for this, so
  there is nothing to compare it with directly. The CMS page only says disagreement happens
  "in rare instances". Our result (under 1% of b jets and about 5% of c jets disagree) fits
  that description.

Why can the two labels disagree at all? A quark and the hadron it turns into do not fly in
exactly the same direction. So, for jets near the edge, the quark ghost can land in one jet
and the hadron ghost in a neighbouring jet, or outside every jet. Then the two labels
differ. This is a normal feature of the method, not a bug in our code.
